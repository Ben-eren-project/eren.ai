Before you rebuild your site,
follow one inquiry.

Check the path before choosing a new page, a better handoff or a different process.

By Eren · September 22, 2026 · A general checklist, not a diagnosis of your business.

On this page
01 / The promise02 / The next step03 / The handoff04 / The follow-up05 / What to measure

A dated site can still work. A polished one can still leave people unsure what to do. Walk the path a customer takes.

1. Can a customer recognize the right service?

On your phone, open the page a customer would land on. Read it as a stranger. Can you tell what you do, who it’s for and whether it fits their need?

Look for: a service description that answers a real buying question. Customers asking about something already listed? Check how it’s explained. That alone won’t prove a redesign raises sales.

2. Is the next step clear and usable?

Tap the main contact, quote or booking link on your phone. Does it go where it should? Is it clear what it asks for and what happens next?

Phone-first suits some businesses, a form suits others. Choose by customer needs, accessibility and how you can respond.

3. Does the inquiry reach a person who owns it?

With the owner’s permission, send a labeled test through the form. Check what the visitor sees and what arrives in your inbox or system. A success message doesn’t prove delivery. A missing one doesn’t prove loss.

Record the time, destination, required details and who owns it. Test any handoff to a booking tool too. Don’t use real customer details.

4. What happens after the first reply?

Follow an inquiry to its next decision: qualified, quoted, booked, declined or waiting. Does it have a named owner and an agreed next action? Ask whoever handles it where they have to remember, retype or chase things.

Slow follow-up is a guess until records or the owner back it up. No set number of messages fits every business. Respect a customer’s choice to stop.

5. Decide what would show the change worked.

Separate a delivery check from a business result. “A labeled test reaching the right inbox” confirms delivery for that test. “More qualified inquiries become bookings” needs reliable before-and-after records and time.

  • Broken link: check the destination on phone and desktop.
  • Handoff: check receipt, required details and a responsible person.
  • Follow-up: record whether the agreed next action happened.
  • Business outcome: compare the same definition over stated periods. Note changes in demand, pricing or capacity.

No records? Start keeping them instead of guessing a conversion rate.

Keep what works.

If the path is clear, inquiries arrive and you can keep up, the site may be fine. Use evidence to pick a change, not to justify one already sold.

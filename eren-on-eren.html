Eren field notes / Our own site

We pointed Eren
at our own website

What it found, where it saw each thing, what it couldn’t tell from the outside, and what we changed.

By Eren · October 9, 2026 · One website, ours. Not a client result.

We don’t have client case studies to show yet, and we won’t invent one. What we can show is what Eren does when it looks at a real website, because we pointed it at ours.

On October 5, 2026, Eren’s evidence tools read a copy of every public page of erenforbusiness.com. Read-only: they didn’t touch the live site, log in or submit anything. For each thing they noticed, they recorded where they saw it. For each thing they couldn’t tell from the outside, they said so. On October 9 we made the changes below.

1. What it found, and what we did

The first step had three different names. Seen on the home page buttons and in the page description: “Start with a free conversation”, “Let’s talk about your business” and “First Look”. A visitor could fairly wonder whether those were three different things.
What we did: one name everywhere. Every button, menu and footer link now says “free First Look”.

Our phone number was hidden. Seen in the page’s structured data, the part written for search engines, and nowhere a visitor could read it. There was no tap-to-call link anywhere on the site.
What we did: the number is now in every footer, with a tap-to-call link.

The request form marked two questions as required. Seen in the page source: “the one problem you want us to diagnose” and “the improvement you’d like us to make” were both marked required. Eren couldn’t tell whether that would block someone who only wanted one of the two, because that depends on a script it doesn’t run.
What we did: only the question for the package you picked is required now.

It couldn’t tell what a visitor sees after pressing send. The page source had no visible thank-you message, and a script handles the rest.
What we did: after a successful send, the thank-you message now takes focus so it can’t be missed on a phone. If a send fails, the page says so and gives the email address and phone number to use instead.

A large video on the home page. It measured the file, 6.3 MB. It did not measure how fast the page loads, so it did not call it a problem.
What we did: nothing on size alone. The film sits behind a play button, and the page loads only enough of it to show the first frame.

2. What it couldn’t tell

Whether a request sent through our form reaches a person. No review from the outside can answer that. Only a real request, sent end to end and watched arrive, can. It’s the first check in our own website inquiry checklist, and it applies to us like anyone else.

How the request form looks on a phone. The form page wasn’t among the pages it rendered at phone width, and it said so. We checked it at phone width while making the fixes above.

3. What it left alone

Every internal link loaded. Every page had a title and a description. The sitemap listed ten pages. It also noted that the only place the site names a location is the About page. That’s deliberate, so we left it.

It recommended no change to any of those. A review that only ever finds problems isn’t one you can trust.

4. What this is, and what it isn’t

This is one website, and it’s ours. It is not a client result and it says nothing about what Eren would find on yours.

It does show how the work is done: Eren records what it saw and where, says what it couldn’t see, and says when to leave something alone. A person reviews it before anything is changed.

None of the fixes was large.

Try the same thing on yours

You can run most of this yourself. Send a request through your own form from your phone. Count the names you use for your first step. Look for your phone number the way a stranger would. The inquiry checklist walks through it.

Why isn’t my website
getting calls?

Five checks to run from your phone in about twenty minutes, before you pay for a redesign or more ads.

By Eren · September 27, 2026 · A general guide, not a diagnosis of your business.

On this page
01 / Tap to call02 / The right service03 / Where you work04 / The form05 / After hours

A quiet phone usually gets blamed on the site’s looks. Sometimes that’s fair. Often one small step between “interested” and “calling” is broken. Run these checks on your phone.

1. Can someone call you with one tap?

On your phone, is your number on the first screen? Does tapping it start a call? If it’s an image or plain text, customers have to copy it. Some won’t.

Look for: a tap-to-call link on the home page and every service page, not only the contact page.

2. Does the page say what you actually do?

Read your home page as a stranger. In a few seconds, can you tell what you do, which jobs you take and who they’re for? “Quality service since 1998” doesn’t tell someone with a leaking water heater that you fix water heaters.

Look for: the services people call about, in their words, near the top.

3. Does it say where you work?

Before calling, someone in the next town wants to know you’ll come. If your site never names the towns you serve, they have to guess. So do search engines.

Look for: the towns you cover, written out in text, not only on a map image.

4. Does the contact form actually reach a person?

Send a test through your own form and note the time. Did it arrive? In which inbox? Who saw it, and when? Old forms often send to a dead address or a folder nobody checks.

Look for: a test that lands in an inbox someone reads daily, and a reply as fast as you’d want one yourself.

5. What happens after hours?

Call your line at 8 pm. What does the caller hear? Can they leave a voicemail, and does anyone check it first thing? If most calls come while you’re on a job or closed, this is often where work slips away.

Look for: a message that says when they’ll hear back, and a habit or simple tool that makes sure they do.

If all five check out

Then the website may not be the problem. It may be how many people find you, your prices or how busy you are. Know that before paying for a rebuild.

How Eren operates

Diagnose the problem.
Agree on the change.

We examine one problem in your business, connect the relevant website and workflow evidence, and review the findings with you. A Build starts only after you agree on the scope, price and checks.

The operating principle
A requested fix is a starting point, not a diagnosis. We test the assumptions behind a redesign, new tool or automation before asking you to commit to it.

Four questions owners ask first.

01

What do I provide?

Your goals, the problems you feel, and whatever evidence you have: your site, how customers reach you, how the work runs today.

02

What do I receive?

Diagnose delivers a decision pack: up to three ranked fixes, supporting evidence, open questions and an action plan. Implementation is a separately agreed Build.

03

What needs my approval?

Anything that sends, spends, publishes or can’t be undone. Nothing reaches your customers until you approve it.

04

What about my data?

It’s used only to do your work. The AI services Eren uses process it to produce your results. It’s never sold or shared for marketing.

Follow one problem through
Illustrative example

A redesign request becomes a question to investigate.

A fictional service-business example. It illustrates how the decision is reached, not a client result.

1

What you bring

A service-business owner asks for a new website because the team keeps chasing customers for missing job details.

2

Agree on the objective

The objective is a usable handover to the team. A website rebuild is one possible response, not an agreed diagnosis.

3

Follow one job through

Website: What does the customer understand and provide?
Workflow: What does the team need to do the job?
Tools: Where should those details be recorded and shared?

Compare the customer’s request, the office record and the handover. Ask what was missing, who added it and whether existing software could carry it through.

4

Compare the options

The evidence might support a clearer service page, a revised intake step or a connection between existing tools. Scope only the change the diagnosis supports.

5

Check the claim

Name the observed gap. Keep its possible business impact separate until there is evidence to support it.

A first draft says the missing details are costing jobs. Nothing yet shows that, so the claim comes out: record the observed gap, mark the impact as uncertain and agree how to measure it.

6

Review, then decide

Ben reviews the findings with the owner. The owner chooses whether to commission a Build, with its price and acceptance checks agreed in advance.

What keeps the work accountable.

Evidence and verification

Findings are checked against the supplied evidence and reviewed by Ben. A separate model adds an independent check when available; model agreement alone is not proof.

Your approval

Ben approves each finding before you see it. Sending, spending, publishing or anything irreversible waits for your go-ahead. What you get is written for a busy owner and ranked by impact.

How it learns

Where data exists, outcomes are recorded and lessons are reviewed before they change how Eren works. No model retrains itself in the background.

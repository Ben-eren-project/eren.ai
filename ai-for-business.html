What does AI for a small business actually do?

Eren adds AI to one defined job in a small business, such as answering staff questions from your own procedures or preparing a first draft from your own records. It works only from sources you approve, a named person on your team checks what it produces, and nothing goes out on its own.

The aim is less repeat work for the people who already do the job, not a new system for them to learn. We start with the tools you already use.

Where AI tends to fit

  • Questions staff keep asking. An assistant that answers only from your written procedures and shows where each answer came from.
  • First drafts from your own records. A reply, a follow-up or a summary prepared from information you already hold, for someone on your team to check and send.

These are examples of the kind of job that suits AI. Whether one fits your business is what a First Look and Diagnose find out.

When AI is the wrong answer

  • The job comes up a few times a month, so setting it up and checking it would take more time than it saves.
  • The information it would need isn’t written down anywhere yet.
  • A mistake would reach a customer before anyone could catch it.
  • A setting in software you already pay for does the job.

In those cases we’ll say so. The better fix is usually a clearer process, a setting you already have, or no change at all.

How it’s set up

  1. One job, agreed in writing, with what goes in and what should come out.
  2. One approved set of sources: your procedures, records or documents, and nothing else.
  3. A named reviewer: someone on your team who checks the output and signs off.
  4. A test set before it goes live: a fixed list of questions, including missing and trick ones, run and written down. No promises about accuracy until that test exists.
  5. Nothing acts on its own. It doesn’t send, pay or decide anything without your approval.

What does it cost?

It starts with a free First Look: a 15-minute call, one real observation and a straight answer on whether more work is worth it. If it is, Diagnose ($750) examines one problem and ranks up to three fixes. An AI Build starts from $1,500, with one fixed price agreed before work starts. Software and AI service costs are always shown separately before you agree.

Questions owners ask

Will AI replace my staff?

That isn’t what we set it up to do. It handles one defined job by preparing answers or drafts, and someone on your team checks them.

Can it send messages to customers by itself?

No. Anything that sends, pays or decides on its own is outside what we build. A person approves what it drafts before anything goes out.

How accurate is it?

We don’t promise a number in advance. Before it goes live, a fixed set of test questions is run, including missing and trick ones, the results are written down, and your reviewer signs off.

Do we have to change the software we use?

No. We start with what you already use. Often a setting or a connection does the job without any AI at all.

Who owns what’s built?

You do. Everything built is yours, with no subscription to us, and your data stays yours and exportable.

Not sure AI fits?

Bring one job that eats your week to a First Look. If AI isn’t the answer, you’ll hear that too.

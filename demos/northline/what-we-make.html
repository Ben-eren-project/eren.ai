What we make

Three kinds of job, each with its own way in.

Most people arrive with one of these in mind. Each one says what to send, what you get back, and goes straight to a quote with the job type filled in.

For engineers and buyers

Production parts to print

Cut, formed and welded parts made from your drawings, in the quantity you need and again when you reorder. The drawing revision is locked on every order, so a reorder matches the last one unless you tell us otherwise.

Typical jobs

  • Brackets, plates and mounting parts
  • Formed covers, panels and channels
  • Welded sub-assemblies for machine builds
  • Repeat orders on a schedule you set
Quote a production part

For maintenance teams

Repair and replacement parts

When a machine is down and the original supplier is gone, send what you can. A photo, a few rough measurements and where it mounts is enough to start. We sketch the part for you to confirm before anything is cut.

Typical jobs

  • Machine guards and covers
  • Brackets, chutes and hoppers
  • Frames and stands
  • Worn parts copied from the original
Describe the part

For builders and installers

Fabricated assemblies New: tube laser

Railings, frames, enclosures and tube structures, cut, welded and finished in the shop. The tube laser cuts copes, miters and slots in one pass, which means fewer joints to fit by hand.

Typical jobs

  • Railings and stair parts
  • Equipment frames and skids
  • Enclosures and cabinets
  • Tube structures with coped joints

Check the tube laser limits before you send long or heavy tube.

Quote an assembly

The decisions behind this page

For the walkthrough. A client site wouldn’t carry this panel.

  1. One section per kind of customer, each with what to send and what comes back. A maintenance manager with a broken guard knows he needs a repair part. He doesn’t know if he needs “forming”.
  2. Every quote button fills in the job type on the quote page, so nobody chooses from a list twice.
  3. Typical jobs, not a machine list. Visitors recognise their own part faster than they recognise a process name.
  4. Drawings instead of photos. A real build uses the shop’s own photos, with permission.
  5. A route for “not sure”, so an unclear job still reaches a person instead of leaving.
